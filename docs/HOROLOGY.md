# A horology primer, using Calibre M-01

This is a short guide to what is happening inside the watch on screen, for anyone who has never opened a mechanical watch. Each section names the part so you can click it in the app.

## 1. Power: the mainspring

A mechanical watch runs on a coiled spring. Turning the **Crown** rotates the **Winding Stem**, which drives the **Crown Wheel**, which turns the **Ratchet Wheel**. The ratchet tightens the **Mainspring** inside the **Barrel**. The **Click** is the small pawl you hear while winding: it rides over the ratchet's saw teeth and stops the spring from unwinding backwards.

As the spring lets down, it turns the barrel's toothed drum once every 8 hours. Calibre M-01 stores **72 hours** of energy. Hold **Wind** in the app to watch the mainspring's coils pack around the arbor.

## 2. The going train: gearing up

The barrel turns slowly and with a lot of force. The **gear train** trades that force for speed:

| Wheel | Speed | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Barrel | 1 turn / 8 h | Stores the energy |
| Centre wheel | 1 turn / hour | Carries the minute hand |
| Third wheel | 7.2 turns / hour | Intermediate step |
| Tourbillon cage | 1 turn / minute | Doubles as the seconds hand |
| Escape wheel | 12 turns / min, relative to the cage | Meters the energy out |

In watchmaking a large gear is a **wheel** and a small one is a **pinion**, whose teeth are called *leaves*. Watch teeth have a distinctive pointed-arch profile, which you can see if you zoom in.

## 3. The escapement: letting energy out one tick at a time

Left alone, the train would spin freely and the spring would unwind in seconds. The **escapement** releases it in precise steps:

- The **Balance** is a weighted wheel that swings back and forth, 3 times a second in each direction. Its **Hairspring** always pulls it back toward centre, and the balance's inertia plus the spring's stiffness set the rhythm, much like a pendulum.
- The **Pallet Fork** (the Swiss lever) rocks between two **banking pins**. Its two **ruby pallet stones** take turns blocking the **Escape Wheel**.
- Each time the balance swings through centre, its ruby **impulse jewel** knocks the fork across. That:
  1. **unlocks** the escape wheel, which recoils slightly;
  2. lets the escape wheel's tooth push the pallet stone (**impulse**), which gives the balance a tiny kick to keep it swinging;
  3. **drops** the wheel half a tooth forward until the other pallet stone catches it (**lock**).

That is one *beat*, one "tick". Calibre M-01 makes **6 beats per second**, or 21,600 vibrations per hour. Set the speed to **1/20×** and select the Pallet Fork to watch it.

## 4. The tourbillon: spinning away from gravity

Gravity pulls slightly differently on the balance depending on how the watch is held, so a watch keeps slightly different time in each position. In 1795 Abraham-Louis Breguet had an elegant idea: put the whole escapement (balance, hairspring, fork and escape wheel) inside a rotating **cage**, so those errors average out over each turn. He patented the **tourbillon** in 1801.

On Calibre M-01:

- The **Fixed Fourth Wheel** is screwed to the main plate and never moves.
- The cage turns once a minute. As it does, the **escape pinion** rolls around the fixed wheel's teeth, and that rolling is what turns the escape wheel.
- It is a **flying** tourbillon: the cage is supported only from below, with no bridge above it, so it seems to float in the dial opening.
- The cage's long arm points at a 60-second track, so it doubles as the **seconds hand**.

## 5. Showing the time: motion works

The centre wheel turns once an hour. The **Cannon Pinion**, friction-fitted on the centre arbor so the time can be set, carries the minute hand. The **Minute Wheel** and **Hour Wheel** reduce 12:36 and then 10:40, a combined 1:12, so the hour hand turns twelve times more slowly.

## 6. Finishing: why it is beautiful

Most of the decoration on a fine movement can never be seen once the case is closed, and making it anyway is the point. The app models the classic finishes:

- **Côtes de Genève:** wavy stripes on the bridges, made by a rotating abrasive wheel.
- **Perlage:** overlapping circles grained onto the main plate.
- **Anglage:** hand-bevelled edges polished to a mirror.
- **Circular graining (soleillage):** sunburst grain on the wheels and ratchet.
- **Heat-blued screws:** steel tempered over a flame until it turns deep blue, with every slot aligned.
- **Jewels in gold chatons:** synthetic rubies used as bearings. Rubies are nearly frictionless and don't wear out.
- **Clous de Paris guilloché:** a hobnail pattern cut into the dial on a rose engine.
